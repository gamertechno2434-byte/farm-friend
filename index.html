What NDVI and EVI mean, in plain words

NDVI — Normalized Difference Vegetation Index

Plants reflect a lot of near-infrared light and absorb red light. NDVI compares those two. A low number means bare soil, dead plants or very weak growth. A high number means a thick, green canopy. It is the simplest and oldest greenness score.

EVI — Enhanced Vegetation Index

EVI uses the same idea but corrects for haze in the air and for very thick leaves. It is often better in humid places and for dense crops. When NDVI and EVI move together, the signal is strong. When they disagree, be careful.

0.0–0.2Bare or very weak. Soil is showing, plants are sparse or dead. Check water, pests and planting.
0.2–0.4Young or weak crop. The field is still filling in. Walk the rows and look for gaps.
0.4–0.6Normal growing crop. The canopy is doing its job. Keep normal care.
0.6–0.9Dense, strong canopy. Good greenness — but watch for disease and plants falling over.

What it cannot do: it cannot name a disease, count plants, see under the leaves, or predict yield. Use it like a smoke alarm: it tells you where to walk.

      The problem

      Data without a decision

      A farmer gets weather in one app and a satellite index in another. “NDVI 0.41” does not say what to do before 8 a.m. Low-literacy farmers are left out of both.

      What we built

      One screen, one action

      A single HTML app with multi-field support, voice readout, colour + shape alerts, NASA MODIS NDVI/EVI, Open-Meteo weather, AI questions and a plant-photo check. Works without a backend in sample mode.

      Why it matters

      Water, heat, inclusion

      Less water wasted, heat damage caught early, weak fields walked sooner, and an interface a non-reader can use. The five-minute morning check replaces three separate tools.

      Weather and place search go straight from the browser. NASA and AI go through the backend so passwords and API keys never sit in the page. The two backends implement the same JSON API, so either can be swapped in.

      Data sources, timing and accuracy
      WeatherOpen-Meteo (built on national models such as ECMWF and GFS). Current conditions plus 7 days. Reliable for the next day, reasonable to 3 days, trend only at 7. Our page refreshes every 15 minutes.
      SatelliteNASA MODIS/Terra, product MOD13Q1.061, layers _250m_16_days_NDVI and _250m_16_days_EVI, 250 m, 16-day composite. NASA processing usually takes 5–30 minutes. New data every 16 days, so it is a monthly check, not live.
      AIGoogle Gemini (default) or OpenAI GPT-4.1, constrained to short, simple sentences. It is told to separate what it sees from what it guesses, never to name a disease from NDVI alone, and never to give chemical doses.
      PlacesOpen-Meteo geocoding, or the phone's own GPS. No account needed.
      Honest limits
      • One satellite value covers a 250 m square, not one plant. A weak pixel may be a corner you never planted.
      • A 16-day composite lags real life. It will not show damage from yesterday.
      • Forecasts beyond three days are a trend, not a promise.
      • AI can be wrong. The photo check cannot diagnose from one picture. It never gives pesticide or fertiliser amounts.
      • No yield predictions. No market prices. No replacement for a soil test or an agriculture officer.
      • Sample satellite numbers in the demo are invented to show the interface; they are always labelled “sample”.
      Privacy, offline and editing
      • Accounts and fields are stored in this browser only. There is no central user database.
      • NASA credentials go to your own backend and are not saved in the page. They are cleared from the form after sending.
      • Plant photos go to your backend and the AI service only when you press Ask.
      • Weather and the last saved forecast stay on the device. Without signal, the app shows the last forecast and the offline plant checklist.
      • The whole interface is one HTML file. Text, colours and tips can be edited in any text editor. The photo block is a generated data block at the top; leave it intact.
      10Straight answers

      Questions people ask

      The practical ones: timing, accuracy, sources, other software, and whether you can change this page.

      Can this app work with other programs or systems?

      Yes. The backend speaks plain JSON over HTTP and allows cross-origin calls. Another system can ask /api/health, start a run with /api/analyze, follow it with /api/status/{id}, read /api/results/{id}, send a photo to /api/photo, or ask a question at /api/chat. Raw numbers are also available as a CSV download.

      How late, or how uncertain, is the information?

      Weather is refreshed every 15 minutes; the next 24 hours are usually close, three days is a good guide, and seven days shows the trend. Satellite greenness is a 16-day composite that can take 5–30 minutes to prepare; it will not show what happened yesterday. So plant health is a monthly check, while weather is a daily one.

      Can users change the HTML or the wording?

      Yes. The app is one HTML file with normal text. Open it in any text editor, change words, colours or tips, save, and open it again. Nothing needs to be rebuilt. Keep the generated photo block and the script section intact.

      Does it update by itself?

      Yes. The weather and alerts refresh every 15 minutes and again when you return to the page. The satellite card keeps the last reading for each field until you run a new analysis; NASA only publishes a new composite every 16 days, so a daily refresh would not add anything.

      Is the information correct? Where does it come from?

      Weather: Open-Meteo. Satellite: NASA AppEEARS using MOD13Q1 v061 layers _250m_16_days_NDVI and _250m_16_days_EVI. Place search: Open-Meteo. AI: Google Gemini. Satellite login: NASA Earthdata.

      All of it is an estimate. The app separates what is measured from what is guessed, and tells you when it is unsure.

      Does it work without internet or without the backend?

      Partly. Tips, the field list, the manual, the plant checklist and the last saved forecast work offline. Live weather needs internet. NASA analysis, AI answers and photo checks need the backend; without it, the Ask box falls back to a basic answer built from the last weather and satellite numbers.

      Are the accounts real? Can I use it as a guest?

      Guest mode is always available. Sign-in is a local convenience: it keeps fields separate for different people on the same phone and is stored only in this browser. It is not a bank-style login and does not follow you to another device.
